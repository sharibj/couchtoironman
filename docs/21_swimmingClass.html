I joined a swimming class

I finally joined a swimming class, and honestly the hardest part was just figuring out how to sign up. The process wasn't obvious and it took me a while. I ended up going through one of the official German websites and landing an intensive course: two weeks, five days a week, ten sessions of 45 minutes each.

The travel was rough. One hour each way for a 45-minute class, so every session cost me about three hours. More than that really, because I was usually wiped afterward and couldn't focus on anything for the next few hours.

The class itself wasn't bad, though I was the worst one in there. Everyone else already knew some kind of stroke. I was the only fully self-taught, no-experience person in the group. I was so scared of the pool that for the whole first week I picked the lane next to the edge so I could grab the wall if I had to. That changed toward the end, when I finally worked out that even if I go under, I can just kick at the water somehow and come back up for a breath. After that I managed to move into the middle of the pool to swim.

It was still a struggle. The pool was 25 meters and most days I couldn't finish a single lap. I'd push off one wall, run out of breath before reaching the other side, stop around the three-quarter mark, and come back.

The trainer was nice. Her whole approach was teaching us exercises we were meant to keep doing after the course ended, the idea being that the more you repeat them the better your form gets. Some of the students were already doing really well. Others struggled with certain drills, but at least they could swim when they had to, they could front crawl. That was the name of my course, by the way: front crawl technique. The criteria were that you shouldn't be afraid of deep water or the pool, and that you should already know some kind of swimming. Neither was true for me. I went anyway.

Breathing was where I struggled the most, and that had a downstream effect on everything else. You're supposed to breathe in through the mouth above the water, then breathe out through the nose as you go under and out through the mouth too, letting all the air out, and finish exhaling through the nose as you come up. After a few strokes I just couldn't do it sometimes. I'd come up with air still in my lungs, so I'd have to breathe out first, which left me no time to breathe in properly. A lot of water went up my nose. I gulped pool water by mistake more than once. It was nasty. I think if I really practiced just the breathing on my own for a few days, I might do a lot better. Maybe that's what I'll have to do.

So the plan is to practice all these drills little by little until I've perfected them, or at least until I'm good at them, before moving on. But that takes consistency and more hours at the pool, and right now I want to focus on the marathon, whose training is going really awful by the way. I'm genuinely afraid I might not be able to finish it, or that I'll get disqualified. But let's see.

A woman from my class was taking diligent notes and graciously shared them with me, so at least I have the techniques written down for whenever I find it in me to go swim and practice on my own. But what will realistically work for me, I think, is a class that meets once a week instead of five times. Five was draining and exhausting, to be honest.

For now, nothing until the marathon. I just want to focus on my work and my running. The swimming classes left a two-week gap in my running, so I need to make up for it somehow. I don't think there's really any way to fully recover lost time in marathon training, but what's done is done. I just need to give it my best.
